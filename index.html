Bet type

Goals bets: Over or Under total goals in the match. One bet per game, 1 unit each.

Double chance: covers two of the three results: home or draw (1X), draw or away (X2), home or away (12). Shorter prices, so they need to win more often.

Which line

Main (2.5 goals): the standard Over or Under 2.5 bet on each game. This is the headline record.

Alt (3.5 goals): the same game's Over or Under 3.5 bet. Tracked separately so it never mixes into the main record.

Near misses: games that almost met our rules, just under the cutoff. Tracked only to learn if the cutoffs are too strict. Not part of the main record.

Over or Under

Record: wins and losses among played bets.

Hit rate: wins divided by played bets.

Units: profit if every bet risked 1 unit at the listed odds. A win pays the odds, a loss costs 1.

ROI: units won per unit risked.

Cumulative units

Running total of units won or lost, day by day, for the bets matching your filters. Tap or hover the line to see a day.

Open vs Filtered

What this compares. Both tracks start from the same list of games. Nothing here is played with real money. We are only collecting results.

Open is the original method with no limits.

Filtered adds limits to see if they help: skip Under bets in the Premier League and LaLiga 2, and build slips with one leg per league, no two legs priced -300 or shorter, and no leg where the model's edge over the book is above 10 points or below zero.

The aim is to learn whether cutting the weaker leagues and the riskiest slip legs makes the results better over a larger sample.

Markov side test

What this is. A separate experiment. It does not touch any pick, record, slip or track above, and nothing is bet on it.

A Markov chain predicts the next step using only where you are now. Here the "square" is the score. The model plays each match minute by minute: each minute a goal can happen, and the chance depends on the current score (the leading team slows down a little, the trailing team pushes a little). It is set so the average total matches our projected total, then we read off the chance of Under 2.5 and Under 3.5.

We compare it with DataGaffer's own chance and a plain Poisson model on finished games. Lower Brier score means better probabilities. Brier of 0.25 is what you get by always saying 50%.
